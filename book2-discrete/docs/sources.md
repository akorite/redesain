## Concrete details carried into the outline

**How each source orders proof-writing instruction.** Velleman organizes proof strategy *by the shape of the goal* (conditional → quantified → conjunction/disjunction → existence/uniqueness), with logic taught first as the grammar. Hammack puts sets→logic→counting first, then devotes one chapter per technique (direct/contrapositive/contradiction) and a chapter to disproof. MIT 6.042J leads with "what is a proof" + implication/iff/cases/contradiction, then well-ordering, then (much later) induction and state-machine invariants. CS70/CS103/15-251 all open with proofs before structures. Our Part I follows the consensus: logic grammar → techniques-by-form → disproof & counterexamples → induction (with WOP as the same idea) → sets/relations/functions as the first "content" domain.

**Exercise types observed.** Velleman/Hammack: short proofs of template form ("prove A⊆B"), error-spotting, English→symbols translation. MIT 6.042J: problem-set proofs plus the WOP/induction templates. Chen (OTIS/English): walkthroughs asking Socratic questions mid-solution; exercises graded IMO 1/4 → 6; a "find the flaw" genre. AoPS archives: AMC multiple-choice (verification of small cases possible) and AIME integer-answer problems (a contest constraint: answer is an integer 000–999 — useful for checkpoint culture). Take for our book: inline exercises mix (a) finish-the-proof, (b) find-the-mistake, (c) compute-small-cases-then-conjecture, (d) AMC/AIME cites.

**Where learners struggle (evidence).** EGMO errata — even a strong author's published proofs missed cases (O inside the triangle; collinear degenerates) and dropped a factor ½: motivates common-mistakes entries on case coverage and constant-checking. Chen's *English* — the fake-solve trap, two-part answers, "citing lemmas" etiquette, time-boxed checking: motivates the exam-writing chapter. OTIS ch.1 — reversibility errors ("if" proved but "only if" assumed), neatness. Cut-the-Knot's fallacy collection — classic "1=2" style algebra slips usable as drill material. Widely attested pain points reflected in the cited materials: negating quantified statements (Velleman ch.2 devotes a section), contrapositive vs converse (Hammack ch.5), induction base/step conflation (6.042J ch.5), overcounting vs inclusion–exclusion (Hammack ch.3, our pool of IE problems), and "at least one" complement handling in probability (CS70 notes sequence defers conditional probability until after counting).
